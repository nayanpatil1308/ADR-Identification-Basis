# ADR-Identification-Basis 
1. Project Title

Identification and Assessment of Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs) from a Pharmacovigilance Perspective

Internship: VirtualWork Lab
Domain: Pharmacovigilance
Area: Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) Identification

2. Objective

The main objective of this task is to understand how Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs) are identified, assessed, documented, and reported as part of pharmacovigilance activities.

This project focuses on the systematic identification of suspected ADRs using patient information, drug history, clinical symptoms, time relationship, dechallenge/rechallenge information, and causality assessment.

3. Introduction

Pharmacovigilance is concerned with the detection, assessment, understanding, and prevention of adverse effects or other medicine-related problems.
An Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) is a harmful and unintended response associated with the use of a medicinal product.
ADR identification is an important pharmacovigilance activity because early recognition of suspected reactions can help improve patient safety and contribute to the detection of medicine safety signals.

4. What is ADR Identification?

ADR identification is the process of determining whether an unwanted clinical event in a patient could be associated with the use of a particular medicine.
The identification process includes:
Recognizing the adverse event.
Identifying the suspected medicine.
Collecting patient and medication information.
Establishing the time relationship.
Considering alternative causes.
Assessing dechallenge/rechallenge information when available.
Performing causality assessment.
Assessing seriousness and severity.
Documenting and reporting the suspected ADR.
Following up for additional information.

7. Important Factors in ADR Identification
    
A. Temporal Relationship

The time between starting the medicine and appearance of the reaction is important.

Example:

Drug started
     ↓
2 days later
     ↓
Skin rash develops

A suitable temporal relationship can support suspicion of an ADR, but it does not prove causality by itself.

B. Dechallenge

Dechallenge refers to the outcome after stopping or reducing the suspected medicine.

Example:

Drug stopped
     ↓
Symptoms improve

This may support a drug-related association.

C. Rechallenge

Rechallenge refers to recurrence of the reaction after re-exposure to the suspected medicine.

Drug given → Reaction occurs
      ↓
Drug stopped → Reaction improves
      ↓
Re-exposure → Reaction occurs again

Intentional rechallenge should generally be avoided when it could place the patient at risk.

D. Alternative Causes

Other possible causes should be considered, such as:

Other medicines
Underlying diseases
Infections
Food or environmental exposures
Other clinical conditions

8. Causality Assessment
   
Causality assessment estimates how likely it is that the suspected medicine caused the reaction.
Common approaches include:
WHO-UMC System
Possible categories include:
Certain
Probable/Likely
Possible
Unlikely
Conditional/Unclassified
Unassessable/Unclassifiable
Naranjo Algorithm
The Naranjo algorithm uses a set of questions to estimate the probability of a drug-event relationship.
The result can be categorized as:
Definite
Probable
Possible
Doubtful

9. Seriousness vs Severity

These terms should not be confused.
Severity
Describes the intensity of the reaction.
Examples:
Mild
Moderate
Severe
Seriousness
Describes whether the event meets important medical/regulatory criteria.
A serious event may involve:
Death
Life-threatening condition
Hospitalization or prolonged hospitalization
Persistent/significant disability
Congenital anomaly/birth defect
Other medically important conditions

10. Example ADR Case
    
Case
A patient is prescribed Medicine A for a bacterial infection.
After two days of treatment, the patient develops a skin rash.

Identification

Suspected drug: Medicine A
Adverse event: Skin rash
Time relationship: Reaction occurred after initiation of therapy
Other medicines: Reviewed
Alternative causes: Considered
Dechallenge: Symptoms improved after discontinuation
Causality: To be assessed using an appropriate causality method
Outcome: Documented and followed up

Pharmacovigilance significance

The case should be documented as a suspected ADR and reported through the appropriate pharmacovigilance reporting pathway when reporting criteria are met.

11. Role of Pharmacovigilance in ADR Identification
ADR identification contributes to:

Patient safety
Detection of new safety signals
Identification of previously unknown reactions
Continuous monitoring of medicines
Benefit-risk assessment
Safer prescribing and medicine use
Regulatory decision-making
Prevention of avoidable medicine-related harm
